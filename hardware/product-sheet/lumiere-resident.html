System

Two computers in one box

The Raspberry Pi is the brain: Lumière Link follows the music and fires your QLC+ show over MIDI, on the Pi itself. The Lumière Node is the hands: it turns the show's Art-Net into DMX. They are separate on purpose. If the Pi ever restarts, the Node keeps the last look for 10 seconds, then fades to the house look, so the room never goes dark.

Lights side, control side

The Pi lies in the back-right corner with its ports facing out, which puts its USB-C on the right wall and its pin header toward the Node, so every jumper wire is short. The Node, its RS-485 module and the XLRs share the left half. Four posts carry the lid; one sits between the two boards because the Pi's USB fills the back-right corner. The fit check in enclosure.py confirms nothing touches, with the Pi's tallest parts included.

Schematic

18 wires, one power source

The Pi's own USB-C supply feeds everything: the Node and the RS-485 module take 5 V from header pins 2 and 4, and every part has its own ground pin so there are no splices. Pin numbers are the Pi's physical header pins.

SolidLive: the show is playing
1 blink / sShow stopped: holding the last look, then fading

FastNo network cable on ART-NET

HOUSE button: press to fade to the house look and stay there; press again for the show. Hold for 3 seconds to store what's on the lights as the new house look (DMX flashes three times). Reading them together: DMX solid with NET blinking means the lights work but the decks aren't connected.

Colour, material, finish

Quiet, dark, and made to sit in a booth

A matte charcoal body that disappears under booth lighting, with the only colour in the engraving and the two status lights. The lettering is cut 0.6 mm deep and rubbed with brass wax, so it reads in the dark without a printed label.

Printing (Bambu P1S)

ASA for the box that lives at the venue (heat and sunlight), PLA for test fits. Textured PEI plate so the lid's top comes out moulded; scarf seam on a back corner; 0.12 mm layers, outer wall at 40 mm/s; scale ASA to 100.5 %. The base prints open side up, the lid top-face down. No supports.

Assembly · in order

From parts to a running box

  1. Press the four M3 inserts into the lid posts with a soldering iron.
  2. Screw the Pi to its four standoffs, ports to the back, USB-C to the right wall. Stick the heatsinks on.
  3. Bolt the XLR socket(s) in from outside. Solder A → pin 3, B → pin 2 and the shield wire → pin 1.
  4. Tape the WT32 onto its rib with its Ethernet socket in the ART-NET opening; tape the MAX485 into its tray. Bridge DE and RE.
  5. Fit the LEDs (with their resistors) and the HOUSE button from the front.
  6. Plug the 18 wires as in the schematic; check 5 V isn't shorted to ground before power.
  7. Screw the fan under the lid's grille, plug it on header pins 1 and 6, close the lid, stick on the feet.
  8. Power up: NET blinks (no decks yet), DMX flashes every 2 s (house look). Connect both RJ45s to the booth switch.
