How Lumière Link works

Step 1 · Connections

Everything on one network switch

Lumière Link listens to the players over the same Ethernet cable DJs already use for Pro DJ Link. No audio cable is needed.

Plug in

  • Each CDJ and the DJM into the switch, from their LINK port.
  • The Mac into the same switch with an Ethernet adapter.
  • Any order is fine, and it works with the app already open.

Good to know

  • Up to 6 players plus the mixer.
  • A plain switch is enough. No router or internet needed.
  • Lightkey runs on the same Mac.
Step 2 · Start up

Open the app and watch the lights

The status lights in the title bar turn green as each device is found. It usually takes a few seconds.

  1. 1Quit PRO DJ LINK Bridge, ShowKontrol or rekordbox on this Mac. Two DJ Link apps on one Mac fight over the players' status.
  2. 2Open Lumière Link. CDJ and DJM lights turn green, and each deck appears in the Decks rack.
  3. 3Press Live at the top right if it isn't lit. Paused holds the clock and all cues.
  4. 4First time only: if macOS asks to let the app find devices on your local network, click Allow.
  5. 5If a CDJ is stuck on an old IP address, the app offers to fix it. macOS asks for your password; nothing changes without it.
Step 3 · Lightkey

Two inputs: tempo and cues

Lumière Link creates two MIDI inputs on the Mac. Set them up in Lightkey once and they stay mapped.

Tempo

  1. In Lightkey's tempo settings choose MIDI Clock.
  2. Pick the input Lumiere Clock.
  3. Lightkey's BPM now follows the master deck, pitch fader included.

Your sequences

  1. Click Sequences above the pads and set how many you made for each pool (up to 24).
  2. Switch on Learn. In Lightkey, start MIDI Learn on a sequence, then click its tile here.
  3. Everything is saved in the Lumière project with the same name as your Lightkey project. You do this once per project, not every show.

Ideas that work well

  • Pre-drop fires one bar before every drop: map it to a blackout or a riser effect.
  • 8 bars fires every eight bars inside a section: use it to step through looks so drops don't go stale.
  • CC 20 carries section energy and CC 23 build progress, for master dimmer or strobe speed.
Step 4 · How it reads the music

Three ways to find drops and breakdowns

For every loaded track the app picks the best source on its own, and the badge on the deck tells you which one it's using.

rekordbox phrases

The DJ analysed the track with phrase detection. Cues land exactly on the beat, ahead of time.

Waveform analysis

No phrase data, so the app reads the waveform the CDJ sends when the track loads. Still on the beat, still no cable.

Live audio

Optional backup for tracks with no rekordbox data at all, from a FOH desk aux. Reacts a moment after it hears the change.

Which deck drives the lights?

Follow → Master (default) follows the tempo master, so a hand-off moves the lights to the new track. On air follows the deck with its fader up. Click a deck in the rack to lock onto it.

Step 5 · During the show

Set it, then leave it

Light delay

If lights look a touch late, turn the Light delay knob up (try 20–40 ms). Cues and the clock fire that much early, so lights land on the beat.

Reading the screen

  • Now / Next show the section and a countdown to the next one.
  • Pads flash as each cue goes to Lightkey; the Cue log keeps a list.
  • Tap any pad to fire it by hand.
